§ — The decision you've made, and the one number that argues back

Buy it all in India. Except you should hear the counter first.

You've decided to buy three months of clothing here rather than shop in Europe, on the basis that European clothes are expensive. That's right for ninety per cent of the list and wrong for exactly one item, and it's worth ten seconds before you commit.

Decathlon in France is not expensive. It's a French company — a Quechua rain shell is €40–60 there, which is roughly Indian pricing, and their alpine kit is rated for actual alpine cold rather than Delhi winter. Grenoble is a mountain city with second-hand ski shops on top of that.

But the timing kills it anyway. Iceland is 25 September and you don't reach Grenoble until 5 October. Every cold-weather item has to be in your bag before you fly, because you need it ten days before you'd ever see a French shop. So: buy in India. Your instinct and the calendar agree, even though the price argument doesn't.

§ — The backpack

Buy a 40-litre cabin-legal pack, and it pays for itself three times over

This is the highest-return purchase on the page, and the reason has nothing to do with comfort.

The arithmetic

You have roughly six budget flights across the semester — Gdańsk→Keflavík, Keflavík→Paris, Lyon→Amsterdam, Porto→Lyon, and both Balkans legs. At €25–40 a checked bag each way, a pack that fits the free cabin allowance saves you €150–250.

A Quechua Travel 500 costs about ₹4,500. It repays itself on the third flight and everything after that is profit — and it removes the baggage carousel from every weekend trip, which on a Sunday-night return before a Tuesday class matters more than the money.

What to look for

  • 40 L, and roughly 45 × 30 × 20 cm. That clears easyJet's allowance outright and Ryanair/Wizz with priority. Bigger than 45 cm on the long side and you're checking it.
  • Front-loading, not top-loading. A clamshell that opens like a suitcase. Top-loaders are for trekking; you'll be living out of this in hostels and unpacking it daily.
  • Lockable zips and a stowable harness. Straps that tuck away survive baggage belts and stop it reading as a hiking pack in a city.
  • Skip the wheels. Cobblestones in Prague, Kotor and Porto, plus stairs in every station without a lift. Wheels also eat 1.5 kg of your allowance.

§ — Footwear

Two pairs, and the sports one shouldn't be a running shoe

You asked for sneakers plus sports shoes. The sneakers are straightforward. The second pair is where the mistake usually happens — a running shoe is the wrong tool for what you're about to do to your feet.

On Nike specifically: their waterproof options are real but thin — the Pegasus Trail GORE-TEX is the one to look at, and it's genuinely good. But for this trip Decathlon's Quechua line does the same job at a third of the price, and Salomon does it better at similar money. Spend the Nike budget on the sneaker, where the brand actually buys you something, and buy the waterproof pair on function alone.

Add flip-flops (₹300). Forty-six nights of shared hostel showers make them non-optional.

§ — Connectivity

Three phases, and one gap that will catch you in November

Two things worth doing regardless

Keep your Indian number alive — ₹200–300/month of minimum recharge. Every Indian bank OTP, your Scapia login, DigiLocker and UPI are welded to it. Losing that number mid-semester is the most disruptive small failure available to you.

Check your phone is eSIM-capable and carrier-unlocked before you buy anything. It's a thirty-second check and the entire plan above depends on it.
