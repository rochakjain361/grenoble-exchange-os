The cheapest travel in Europe, and your pass already covers it

This block exists because two facts line up. Your attendance rule turns out to be 17 of 20 — you can miss three per course, so a mid-week absence is affordable. And the Eurail Global Pass covers 33 countries including Slovenia, Croatia, Serbia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Montenegro and North Macedonia — the whole region, already paid for.

Why November, and what it costs you

It absorbs the Zurich and Strasbourg windows (20–23 and 27–30 Nov), which is the real price of admission. Move Zurich up to G02, 16–19 October — it's a weekend-only trip, so it clears the Eco-Design intensive without touching a weekday — and you keep the €120 that weekend returns on the pass. Strasbourg's Christmas markets are the genuine loss.

Everything after stays intact: you're back for Fête des Lumières on 4–7 December, the ski weekend, and the last fortnight of term. And the pass expires around 10 December, so November is the last window where the Balkans ride for free.

§ — Before you book

Three things that could ruin this trip

1 · Validate your visa with OFII before you leave Schengen. Serbia, Bosnia and Montenegro are outside the Schengen area, so this trip takes you out and brings you back in. A French long-stay visa must be validated with OFII after arrival to function as a residence permit — and re-entry on an unvalidated VLS-TS is where people get stopped at the border. Do the OFII validation in your first weeks in Grenoble, in October. It is an online formality if you do it early and a genuine emergency if you do it at Belgrade airport.
2 · Balkan rail is slow, and the pass is not always the right answer. Belgrade to Sarajevo is the clearest case: theoretically covered, practically a bad day. Take the €25 bus. The pass is worth having here for Ljubljana–Zagreb–Belgrade and the Sarajevo–Mostar run, not for completeness. Fighting to use it on every leg will cost you more in hours than it saves in euros.
3 · You'll have missed roughly seven classes by the end of November. Three for Iberia, four here. That is comfortably inside 17 of 20 — but it is concentrated in the back half of term, when group projects consolidate and hand-ins cluster. Check what's due in late November before you commit, and tell the professors in October rather than emailing from Sarajevo.

Worth knowing

Time in Serbia, Bosnia and Montenegro doesn't count against your Schengen allowance, because none of them are in it. Irrelevant while you hold a French residence permit, but useful to know.

If you want a sixth country, North Macedonia is also on the pass — Skopje sits a night train from Belgrade and would extend this to twelve days. And the Belgrade–Bar line through Montenegro, 11 hours across 435 bridges and tunnels, is one of the most spectacular railways in Europe and is fully covered. It's an alternative to the Sarajevo routing rather than an addition — but if you want one train journey from this semester that you'll actually remember, that's the one.
